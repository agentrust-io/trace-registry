## 3. Verify it yourself

The receipts verify with the registry's own command (pip install "trace-verify[witness]"):

    trace-verify receipt --checkpoint state/checkpoints/<entry hash>.json --response state/receipts/<entry hash>.post.json \
      --expected-log-id trace-registry/v1 --registry-key bc133259c094f63694b4ec48a295d7501a9a0cd536df5631fb4663c155f7bc90 \
      --witness-key c7c47d073a2c8b348d12c7207aaf7a403f5a3c2695227bf8ed2963ff52f98baf

The files are in ogasurfproject-jpg/horizon-shield under workers/hs-ledger/nenrin/registry-witness-v0/. witness.py verify rechecks the whole state against a clone of the registry.

## 4. The honest boundary

This record fixes when the witness key and these receipts existed: no later than the Bitcoin block this ledger entry is stamped to. It does not certify anything the registry's records claim. It does not show that every observer saw the same registry; that needs other witnesses and someone comparing them. A key holder can always sign a second history. What makes one detectable is this: every tree head is published, and the first one is fixed here. Two heads under this key that no consistency proof joins are proof of misbehaviour.
